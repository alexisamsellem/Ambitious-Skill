## What the losses have in common

1. **The claims go past the evidence** (cases 1 and 3). Codex's findings on (b), checked by the evaluator:
   - Case 1: the deck "retains chance, provisionally" (p = 0.082) and "rejects" a support cause because aggregate tickets are low. A non-significant test does not show chance, and a stable aggregate does not rule out a cause at account level. **Confirmed: the wording is stronger than the evidence.**
   - Case 1: "thresholds fixed a priori on October 2024 to March 2025". That window contains the January to March 2025 ticket spike the alert is meant to catch, so the backtest is not independent. **Confirmed.** The flaw comes from the rewritten prompt itself ("set the thresholds only on data before April 2025"), so it is a flaw of the skill, not of the executor.
   - Case 1: base rate "1.01 % per month" before Q2 2025. The data gives 4 churns over 466 customer-months, 0.86 %. **Not reproduced.**
   - Case 1: "+4.58 %, the weakest growth of the last 7 quarters". Q1 2026 was +4.65 %, so the claim is true. **Codex was wrong.**
2. **Targets were set without checking what the input allows** (case 3). The rewrite asked for LCP ≤ 200 ms on desktop and a 150 KB first view. The images are random noise, which barely compresses, so those targets were physically out of reach. The executor said so honestly, but the run spent its effort on targets that could not be met.
3. **The output is heavy for its reader** (cases 1 and 3): "disproportionate", "excessive complexity". In case 1, the deck has 1 876 words, against 897 for the raw version.
4. **The proof was not in the deliverable** (cases 1 and 3). Codex judged the scripts and reports "announced but absent". This is **partly an evaluator error**: only the answer and the site were copied into the judge folder, for every variant. The verdicts are kept as they are, with no post-hoc re-run.
