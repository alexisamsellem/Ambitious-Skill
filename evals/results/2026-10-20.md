**Findings checked by the evaluator:**
- `/ambitious` found a real script-level defect. 26 of 48 batch/hub rows target a version older than the hub's recorded package, and `assemble` on such a batch ends in a SAFE STOP ("question text differs from durable registry"); reproduced on PHP. Its `resolve` marks all 26 as superseded, and its proofs re-run green: 240 tests, replay PASS on 48 rows and on 165 packages.
- No operator fell into that trap in S7, though. The original skill, read by a careful operator, already avoids resuming the superseded batch. So the defect is real but its harm was not reproduced at the operator level.
- `/ambitious` introduced one regression. `resolve "health"` returns `new` instead of listing the two hubs, because it matches only exact names or slug prefixes. An operator following it would treat a partial name as a new company. Reproduced.
- `doctor --offline` accepts a batch that does not exist (exit 0). The "doctor before the batch exists" defect several variants reported is not real.

**Reading:** with real access and an executed judge, no variant improves on the original agent's behaviour in these 7 scenarios, and `/ambitious` is the only one to regress (S2). That **contradicts** the evaluator's claim of the same day that the paper judge was unfairly penalising ambition on existing skills. On case 7, the paper judge and the executed judge point the same way. The earlier claim stands only for cases 1 to 3, where objective judges did favour `/ambitious`.

**Limits:** one agent; Sonnet operators (real runs use a stronger model); no network, Chrome or sub-agents; 4 pre-registered scenarios and 3 post-hoc ones; S1 mis-specified.
